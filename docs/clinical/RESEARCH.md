# Active-Rehab DATA_GAPS — sourced research pack

Companion research for `DATA_GAPS.md` (PR #11, osteo29/Active-Rehab).
Compiled 2026-11-23. Every item below is quoted verbatim (lightly trimmed, marked with ellipses)
from the cited source. Nothing here is paraphrased clinical content invented for the project.
Where a source could not be found, that is stated explicitly — do not fill those gaps by copying
content between conditions.

Source-quality tiers used:
- Hospital / health-system rehabilitation protocols (Massachusetts General Brigham, Lahey Hospital
  & Medical Center, NHS trusts, Sanford Health, UW Health, IU Health, Cody Regional Health, Andrews
  Sports Medicine, Aspetar)
- APTA/JOSPT Clinical Practice Guidelines (peer-reviewed)
- Peer-reviewed reviews (PMC/BJSM/JOSPT journals)
- Professional references (StatPearls, MSD Manual Professional)
- Secondary/clinic sources are flagged as such where used.

## Part A — DATA_GAPS section 3: protocols 71–85, empty progression criteria (phases 1–2)

### [71] Cubital Tunnel Syndrome
Source: "Conservative Treatment for Cubital Tunnel Syndrome" (US hand-therapy protocol PDF, mirrored
at orthosurgery.gr — hosting site is a third-party mirror; the document itself is an occupational-
therapy conservative-care protocol). Supporting NHS patient source: East Sussex MSK leaflet.
- URL: http://www.orthosurgery.gr/plirofories/protokola/elbow/Conservative%20Treatment%20for%20Cubital%20Tunnel%20Syndrome.pdf
- URL: https://eastsussexmsk.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Cubital-Tunnel-Syndrome-leaflet-NHSE-London-FINAL.pdf

Exact passages (protocol):
> "DURATION: Average estimate of formal treatment 1-3 times per week up to 10 visits over 4 weeks
> based on Occupational Therapy evaluation findings."
> "If patient presents with the following Self-Management Criteria: Good understanding and execution
> of home exercise program. Minimal to no limitation in active range of motion of elbow/forearm/wrist.
> Minimal to no edema at elbow. then patient can be placed on a home exercise program in conjunction
> with a splint wearing schedule."
> "DISCHARGE CRITERIA: Full elbow, forearm, and wrist active range of motion. Independent with
> comprehensive home exercise program. Patient has adequate knowledge of diagnosis and demonstrates
> ability to self-manage symptoms."
> "WEEK ONE TO FOUR: GOALS: ... Patient will be knowledgeable in activities and postures to avoid:
> Repetitive flexion/extension at elbow or wrist. Resting elbows on hard surfaces. Prolonged elbow
> flexion. ... Ulnar nerve glides. Home exercise program done 3-4 times per day."

Mapping: phase 1–2 progression can be expressed as the self-management criteria above (minimal/no
AROM limitation, minimal/no edema, independent HEP) before moving from splinted protection + nerve
glides to progressive loading.

### [73] Trigger Finger (post-injection / post-op)
Source: Ashford and St Peter's Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, "Post-Operative Advice: Trigger Finger
Release" (Hand Therapy, v3, June 2024).
- URL: https://ashfordstpeters.info/images/leaflets/hand-therapy/HN107-Hand-Therapy-Trigger-Finger-Release-Post-Op-Jun-2024.pdf

Exact passages:
> "Complete the following sequence of exercises and hold each position for 10 seconds. Repeat up to
> 10 times 3-4 times a day." (immediately post-op)
> "10 - 14 Days After Surgery ... Sutures are normally removed 2 weeks following surgery. ... If you
> notice that your strength is still weak. Practise squeezing a soft sponge or soft stress ball; avoid
> squeezing anything too hard as this may cause pain. It is recommended to use your hand for light
> activities and gradually increasing to heavier activities as able / comfortable."
> "After Two Weeks: Please seek a referral to our department if you are unable to make a full fist at
> this stage, have persistent swelling, pain and/or difficulty straightening your finger."

Mapping: progression criteria = full active fist by ~2 weeks, swelling resolving, full finger
extension; escalate if absent.

### [74] Mallet Finger
Source: Worcestershire Acute Hospitals NHS Trust, "Extensor Tendon Mallet Injuries (Zone I & II)
Conservative treatment" (WAHT-OCT-034, v2, approved 18 June 2025). Supporting: Calderdale &
Huddersfield NHS FT patient leaflet.
- URL: https://apps.worcsacute.nhs.uk/KeyDocumentPortal/Home/DownloadFile/3390
- URL: https://plr.cht.nhs.uk/download/411/Mallet%20Splint%20Instructions%20A4

Exact passages (WAHT):
> "Immobilisation phase: Bony mallets 6 weeks; Soft tissue mallets 8 weeks"
> "Patient to be fitted with stack splint in order to keep DIP-joint in neutral position. Splint to be
> worn continuously and to be removed only for skin care."

Exact passages (CHT):
> "Bony Mallet: Continuous splinting for 6 weeks. Soft Tissue Mallet: Continuous splinting for 8
> weeks."
> "Weaning Phase: After the initial 6 or 8 weeks, you will likely be advised to wear the splint at

Mapping: phase-1→2 progression = completion of continuous splinting period (6 vs 8 weeks by injury
type) without extensor lag, then weaning.

### [75] Boutonniere Deformity
Source: StatPearls "Boutonniere Deformity" (NCBI Bookshelf). Supporting: chronic boutonniere
conservative protocol (orthopaedic hand-surgeon protocol document; hosted on a private surgeon site —
secondary provenance).
- URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470323/
- URL: https://korshjafarniamd.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Chronic-Boutonniere-Deformity.pdf

Exact passages (StatPearls):
> "The splint should be worn full-time for 6 to 8 weeks to allow central slip healing, with strict
> avoidance of PIP flexion during this period to prevent disruption of early repair. Daily passive
> and active flexion and extension of the free DIP joint should be performed. After the initial 6 to
> 8 weeks, the splint should be worn at night only for an additional 4 to 6 weeks."

Exact passages (chronic protocol):
> "6-8 WEEKS: Initiate gentle AROM/PROM to the PIPJ 4-6/day, 20 repetitions. ... If there is an
> extension lag greater than 20 degrees, delay passive flexion or dynamic flexion splinting of the
> PIPJ"
> "12 Weeks: Resume full ADL's. May continue night splinting for up to 6 months post initiation of
> treatment if an extension lag greater than 20 degrees is present."

### [76] Flexor Tendon Repair (Zone II)

Exact passages (phase goals = progression criteria):

> "Phase 2: 11-14 days post-op. Goals: Involved finger(s) will have up to 1/2 active fist by the end
> of 2 weeks"
> "Phase 3: 15-28 days post-op (3-4 weeks). Goals: The involved finger(s) will have a 3/4 active

> "Phase 5: 6-7 weeks p-op. The Manchester Orthosis is discontinued by 6 weeks ... Gentle

> "Phase 6: 7-10 weeks p-op. Grip strengthening may be continued or initiated ... Education regarding

### [77] Extensor Tendon Repair
Source: Cody Regional Health, "Hand Therapy Following Extensor Tendon Repair" (hand-therapy
protocol).
- URL: https://www.codyregionalhealth.org/documents/content/Hand-Therapy-Extensor-Tendon-Protocol.pdf

Exact passages:
> "3 days following repair: ... Create dynamic extension splint with flexion block ... Patient should
> flex to flexion block 20 times per hour when awake"
> "2 weeks post op: ... Begin passive or active flexion of PIP & DIP joints only while supervised by

> "4 weeks post op: Remove flexion block ... Start active 'hook fist'"

> "8 weeks post op: D/C resting splint. Allow resisted extension & full strengthening"
> "12 weeks post op: Full strength attained. Activity unrestricted"

### [79] Thumb UCL Sprain (Skier's Thumb)
Sources: MSD Manual Professional Edition (reviewed Oct 2025); peer-reviewed review "Injury to Ulnar
Collateral Ligament of Thumb" (J Hand Surg Asian-Pac, PMC6583257).
- URL: https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/injuries-poisoning/sprains-and-other-soft-tissue-injuries/ulnar-collateral-ligament-sprains

Exact passages (MSD):
> "Initial treatment is immobilization with a thumb spica splint ... for 4 weeks. After a few weeks

### [81] Quadriceps Strain (Grade II) and [83] Rectus Femoris Strain
Source: "Diagnosis and management of quadriceps strains and contusions", Current Reviews in
Musculoskeletal Medicine (PMC2941577).
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2941577/

Exact passages:
> "Practically, the first 24–72 h after quadriceps strain should be focused on the RICE principles."
> "The acute phase of treatment is subsequently followed by an active phase of management once the
> injured leg is recovering well. This phase usually begins approximately 3–5 days after the initial
> injury depending on its severity. Stretching, strengthening, range of motion, maintenance of
> aerobic fitness, proprioceptive exercises, and functional training are the primary components of
> this phase. ... Strengthening exercises can begin gradually and should progress sequentially through
> isometric, isotonic, isokinetic, and functional exercises."
> "Practically, athletes should have normal knee range of motion, be pain free, demonstrate near
> normal strength compared to the contralateral side and perform well on functional field tests."
> "Isokinetic muscle strength testing can be a useful tool for assessing strength and guiding return
> to sports."

Note: the review states "There are no established consensus guidelines or criteria for safe return
to sports following muscle strains" — the criteria above are the authors' practical criteria, not a
consensus standard. Also relevant: 2025 JSAMS systematic review "A lag in quad research. A
systematic review of criteria for rehabilitation progression and return to play following quadriceps
muscle strain injury" (https://www.jsams.org/article/S1440-2440(25)00228-2/pdf), which documents
how thin the quad-specific criteria literature is. A clinician should see this honesty reflected in
the data.

### [84] Gluteal Muscle Strain
Source: Andrews Sports Medicine, "Gluteal Strain" patient/protocol sheet (The Sports Medicine
Patient Advisor series).
- URL: https://www.andrewssportsmedicine.com/storage/wysiwyg/120-_gluteal_strain.pdf

Exact passages (return-to-sport criteria, quoted from the document):
> "You may safely return to your sport or activity when, starting from the top of the list and
> progressing to the end, each of the following is true: You have full range of motion on the injured
> side compared to the uninjured side. You have full strength of the injured side compared to the
> uninjured side. You can jog straight ahead without pain or limping. You can sprint straight ahead
> without pain or limping. You can do 45-degree cuts, first at half-speed, then at full-speed. You
> can do 20-yard figures-of-eight, first at half-speed, then at full-speed. You can do 90-degree
> cuts, first at half-speed, then at full-speed. You can do 10-yard figures-of-eight, first at
> half-speed, then at full-speed. You can jump on both legs without pain and you can jump on the leg
> on the injured side without pain."

Exact passages (exercise progression):
> "You can stretch your gluteal muscles right away. You can begin strengthening your gluteal muscles
> as soon as the sharp pain goes away and you only have a dull ache using exercise 3, gluteal
> isometrics. After gluteal isometrics become easier, you can do gluteal strengthening exercises 4,
> 5, and 6. After the gluteal strengthening exercises become easy, strengthen your buttock muscles
> by doing lunges, exercise 7."
(Exercises listed: single knee to chest stretch; hamstring stretch; gluteal isometrics; prone hip
extension; resisted hip extension; hip abduction; lunge.)

### [85] Tibial Stress Fracture (Return to Running)
Sources: The Iowa Clinic (Christopher Kim, MD) "Tibia Stress Fracture / MTSS Rehab Protocol";
Bolthouse, "Return to Running After a Tibial Stress Fracture: A Suggested Protocol", Orthopaedic
Practice (OPTP); 2024 scoping review (JOSPT Open / PMC11393297) — identified but full text not
retrievable in this pass (title/URL only, do not quote).
- URL: https://www.iowaclinic.com/Content/cms/documents/Services/Orthopaedics/Tibia-Stress-Fracture-MTSS-Rehab-Protocol.pdf
- URL: https://www.orthopt.org/uploads/content_files/Downloads/OPTP/27_1/Return_to_Running_Bolthouse.pdf
- URL (review, unquoted): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393297/

Exact passages (Iowa Clinic):
> "Stage 1: Offload Involved Extremity (~2 weeks). Criteria for Advancement: Pain-free daily walking
> without WB restriction"
> "Stage 2: Graded Return to Activity / Exercise. Criteria for Advancement: Pain-free progression
> through graded exercise without symptom recurrence or regression in aerobic capacity."
> "Stage 3: Return to Sport / Full Function. Criteria for Advancement: Pain-free sport specific,
> activity specific, and plyometric training without symptom recurrence or regression in aerobic
> capacity."
> "Graded Running Program: Start at 50% of pre-injury intensity, increase 10-15% each week and
> reference soreness rules to guide progressions."
> MRI grading table (Fredericson criteria, as reproduced in the protocol): Grade 1 "Return to running
> in as short at 2-4 weeks"; Grade 2 "Return to running suggested in 4-6 weeks"; Grade 3 "Return to
> impact activity suggested 6-9 weeks"; Grade 4 "Recommends casting for 6 weeks and return to impact
> activity after additional 6 weeks."
> Soreness rules (adapted from Fees et al. 1998): "Soreness during warm-up that continues: 2 days
> off, drop down 1 step ... No soreness: Advance 1 step per week or as instructed by healthcare
> professional"

Also covers protocol [86] Medial Tibial Stress Syndrome (this document's stated diagnosis is MTSS).
Caution per the same document: "It is imperative to differentiate anterior tibial stress injury
(high risk stress fracture) and posterior medial tibial stress injury (low risk stress fracture)"
— for [87] Femoral Neck Stress Reaction (high-risk) this tibial document is NOT applicable; see
Part D.

### [32] SLAP Lesion (Conservative)

### [34] Supraspinatus Tear (Partial — Conservative) and [10] Shoulder Impingement

> "...for pain, disability, and readiness to return to sport, along with functional performance
> measures to guide the return-to-sport continuum and determine timelines for return to sport."

### [49] Baker's Cyst — GAP CONFIRMED
NHS (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/bakers-cyst/) and NICE CKS (https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/bakers-cyst/)

### [51] Knee Osteoarthritis (Conservative) — partially a gap

### [95] Perthes Disease — timing is disease-stage based, not week based
Source: Bone & Joint Journal 2024, "Clinical consensus recommendations for the non-surgical

Mapping: phase timing for Perthes should key off radiographic disease stage (early vs late), not
